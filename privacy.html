Privacy

Resenha works with any Atmosphere account, and it can also create one for you on its own server. If you make your account there, Resenha’s server holds it, as described below. Otherwise your account and everything in it stay with your own host. The app sends usage analytics and crash reports, described under “Analytics and crash reports”. Its website, resenha.ink, shows published reviews by reading them from their writers’ public accounts when someone opens them. The only things the website keeps are the language you choose on its home page and, if you pick one, its appearance, light or dark. Both are saved in your own browser, so the site opens the same way next time.

Accounts on Resenha’s server

If you create your account on Resenha’s server, pds.resenha.ink, it stores your email address, a hash of your password (never the password itself), your handle, your account identifier, and everything you publish or add to the account: reviews, shelves, posts, likes, follows and files. Like any Atmosphere account, what you publish is public.

The server is run for Resenha by Protobase, as its hosting provider. Protobase keeps encrypted backups of the server for a limited time, at most 90 days, after which they are deleted. The server’s logs hold IP addresses for a short period, to keep it running and to stop abuse.

Your email is used only to confirm your address, reset your password, send the code that confirms deleting your account, and tell you about your account. Never for marketing.

When you sign up, Resenha checks that the request comes from a genuine copy of the app, using Apple’s App Attest. Apple confirms the app is genuine, and resenha.ink receives that confirmation. It keeps no identifier of your device. Your email and password go straight from the app to the server, never through resenha.ink. To prevent abuse, resenha.ink counts sign-ups from each internet address. It keeps only a scrambled form of the address, never with your handle or email, and deletes it after an hour.

Accounts are for people 16 or older.

You can delete your account in the app, from My Account. Deleting it removes it from Resenha’s server: your library, shelves, reviews, posts, files and email. Apps that copied your public posts and reviews, such as Bluesky or BookHive, may keep their copies, and your account identifier’s public history stays in the network’s directory, as the protocol requires. Backups expire on the schedule above.

Accounts on Eurosky and Bluesky

Resenha can also create your account on Eurosky or Bluesky. They ask everyone who signs up to pass a check on their own page, shown inside Resenha. Resenha doesn’t see what you do on that page. Your account then lives with that host, under its own privacy policy and terms, and Resenha keeps nothing of it.

On your devices

Signing in keeps a session in the keychain on that device, so you stay logged in. Signing out removes it.

Drafts stay on the device. If iCloud is on, they also sync through your iCloud to your other devices. They are not published, and Resenha does not upload them anywhere else.

When you unpublish a review, its text goes back to being a draft, kept the same way, until you delete the draft.

When you publish

Publish saves the review to your own Atmosphere account, the same account you use with BookHive. From then on it belongs to that account. Resenha does not keep a copy.

The review is saved twice. Formatted, as a Standard.site article, which resenha.ink and other reading apps show. And as plain text on the book’s BookHive record, so BookHive shows it without formatting marks. Resenha also saves a small record that links the review to its book, and a picture of the book’s cover for its link card. The review’s title, whether you marked it as having spoilers, and the languages it’s written in are saved with it. The first time you publish, you choose where the review goes: a Book Reviews publication Resenha creates for you, named after your profile and with Resenha’s icon, or one of your existing Standard.site publications. All of it is public, like the review.

If “Post to the Atmosphere” is on, Resenha also posts from your account, with a link to the review. The post appears on Bluesky, Mu Social and other apps that read the Atmosphere. Replies to that post are the review’s comments, and a comment you write in Resenha is posted as a reply from your account. Unpublishing removes the review, its link and the Bluesky post that announced it from your account. Replies other people wrote stay in their own accounts.

resenha.ink is hosted by Netlify, which, like any web host, handles the requests of the people who visit it.

To sign in, your password is entered on your account host’s page, such as Bluesky’s or Resenha’s server’s. When you create an account in the app, the password you choose goes straight to the host you picked. It never passes through resenha.ink.

Shelves

Shelves are saved to your own Atmosphere account, like reviews, in the format BookHive and Popfeed use, so they show in those apps too. They are public: anyone can see a shelf’s name and the books on it. Deleting a shelf removes it from your account.

Readers’ profiles show their shelves, which Resenha reads from their public accounts.

Following and the Parlor

Following a reader saves a follow to your own Atmosphere account. It is the same follow Bluesky and BookHive use, so following or unfollowing in Resenha does the same there, and like other follows it is public.

From a reader’s profile you can like, reply to, repost and quote their posts. Each is saved to your own Atmosphere account as a Bluesky like, reply, repost or post, the same as in Bluesky, and like those it is public. Undoing a like or a repost removes it from your account.

Adding a friend, by scanning their code or opening their link, follows them and saves a small friend record to your own account naming them, so their app can show it as a friend request. Like follows, both are public. Accepting a request follows back. Your notifications are read from your account to show friend requests, new followers and comments on reviews; which ones you’ve seen or dismissed is kept on your device, never sent anywhere.

The Parlor shows what the people you follow started and finished. To build it, Resenha asks BookHive for their public profiles, and keeps a copy on the device so it opens quickly. Signing out deletes it.

Reading reviews

Community reviews come from BookHive’s public feed. To show their writers’ names, Resenha looks them up in Bluesky’s public directory. To know whether a review is marked as having spoilers, and the languages it’s in, Resenha reads the public review links in its writer’s account. Resenha keeps copies of these on the device so lists open quickly. Nothing about you is sent with these requests.

eBooks and comics

Books and comics you add, as EPUB, PDF or CBZ files, are uploaded to your own Atmosphere account and saved there with a small record naming the file and, if you connect it, the book it is. Like everything in an account, the file is public: anyone can download it. Removing a book from eBooks deletes its record, which lets your account host delete the file. A copy of each book you open is kept on the device so it opens quickly, and is removed when space is needed.

Where you are in a book, with any highlights you make, is kept on the device and, if iCloud is on, in your iCloud, like drafts and Kobo highlights. It is never published.

Comics are read from their creators’ own accounts, and the list of comics on the network comes from resenha.ink, which reads it from the network every hour; the request to resenha.ink carries nothing about you. Subscribing to a series, liking a chapter or commenting on one is saved to your own account, publicly, the way a follow or a reply is, in the format sequential.work apps use. How many people liked or commented on a chapter, and its comments, are looked up in Constellation, a public index of the network; so are replies to your comments, for your notifications. When a comic’s creator or commenter is shown, their name and picture come from Bluesky’s public directory.

Japanese books and reviews are set in a font Resenha downloads from resenha.ink the first time it’s needed, which is a request to resenha.ink’s host, Netlify, like opening the site.

Scanning a barcode

On iPhone and iPad, Resenha can use the camera to read a book’s barcode. The picture is read on the device and is never saved or sent anywhere. Only the number it finds, the book’s ISBN, is used to search BookHive’s catalog.

When you report or block

A report is sent from your account to the network’s moderation service, Bluesky’s, with the review, post or account you reported. A block is saved to your own account, where other apps can see and respect it.

Resenha keeps a list of what you blocked and reported on the device, to hide it. It also downloads a public list of content its maintainer removed after reports. Nothing about you is sent with that request.

Kobo highlights

When you import from a Kobo, Resenha reads a copy of the e-reader’s database on your device. Nothing on the Kobo is changed. Your highlights and notes are kept on the device and, if iCloud is on, in your iCloud, like drafts. Unlike reviews, which are public, they are never published, and never sent to Resenha, BookHive or Bluesky, unless you quote one in a review you publish. To match your books, Resenha searches BookHive’s catalog with their titles, authors and ISBNs, nothing else.

Videos

Book pages search YouTube for reviews and interviews about the book. The search sends the book’s title and author to Google’s YouTube API, with nothing about you. Videos play in YouTube’s privacy-enhanced player, and Google’s privacy policy applies when you play one.

Analytics and crash reports

The app sends usage events and crash reports to PostHog, an analytics service, on its servers in the European Union, so we can see which features are used and fix what breaks. The events are: opening and leaving the app, the screens shown, publishing, updating or unpublishing a review, removing a book from your library, creating a shelf and adding a book to one, and starting and finishing a library import, with the number of books. Crash and error reports carry technical details of what went wrong. Each comes with your device’s model, its system version and Resenha’s version, and is linked to your account identifier and handle, so a problem can be traced to the account it happened in. PostHog doesn’t store your IP address.

None of it includes what you write or read: no review text, book titles, shelf names, files or highlights.

Before you sign in, events carry a random identifier instead of your account. Signing out starts a new one. The app has no setting to turn analytics off yet. To have the analytics linked to your account deleted, ask through the link at the bottom of this page; your handle is all we need. PostHog processes this data for us and doesn’t use it for anything else.

What we don’t do

No ads. No tracking across other apps and websites. We don’t sell data, and we don’t share it with anyone beyond the services named here.

Sending feedback

Feedback you send from Resenha is posted from your account to Resenha’s public board on userinput.app, with any screenshots you add. Like a review, it is public, anyone can read it, and it stays in your account.
